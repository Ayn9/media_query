The Return to the Moon: Artemis and Beyond

For the first time since Apollo 17 touched down in December 1972, NASA's Artemis program is steering humanity back to the lunar surface — and this time, to stay. Unlike the Cold War-era space race, Artemis is built on international partnerships, commercial innovation, and a long-term vision of sustainable lunar presence.

The Lunar Gateway — a small space station that will orbit the Moon — will serve as a staging point for surface missions and eventually as a waypoint for crewed Mars expeditions. Engineers are already testing the habitats, life-support systems, and in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) techniques that will allow astronauts to extract water-ice from the lunar south pole.

If the program succeeds, the Moon will transform from a destination into a departure lounge — humanity's first permanent foothold beyond Earth, and a proving ground for the 33-million-mile voyage to the Red Planet.
